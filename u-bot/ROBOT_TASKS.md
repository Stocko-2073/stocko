# Yard tasks people actually want

Source: the reply thread on <https://x.com/_Stocko_/status/2094184323075150075>
("if you had a small autonomous robot working in your yard, what tasks would
you want it to do?"), 6.2k views, 89 replies from other people. Plus "tree
branch trimming", which came up in a separate thread.

Jokes are filtered out -- no "shoot the intruders", no sisyphean hole-digging.
Worth knowing that jokes attract likes: the highest-liked reply in the whole
thread is @HoffmanTactical's "Waiting with weapon." at 10. Leaving the jokes in
would have distorted the ranking badly.

**Scoring.** Rank is `askers + likes`. One person asking and one person liking
each count as one vote. Three caveats:

- **One row per task, not per tweet.** A reply naming four jobs is counted into
  all four. So @ai_christianson's 12-like reply -- "string trimming, weeding
  gardens, picking up sticks, removing leaves" -- puts 12 likes behind each of
  those four rows independently. Rows that came from a single reply naming
  several tasks therefore carry identical scores, which is correct: one person
  wanted all of them equally.
- X's cached like counts lag by a minute or two. Treat single-digit counts as
  approximate; the ordering is stable, the exact numbers are not.
- Below a score of about 5 the ordering is noise. Those rows are a menu, not a
  ranking.

The **Theme** column groups tasks that share hardware or a perception problem;
the notes further down are organised by theme rather than by row.

### What counting likes changed

At the theme level, the top five -- weeding, mowing, debris, animals, insects
-- do not move whether you count asks alone or asks plus likes. The head of the
list is robust to how you score it.

Two themes move up out of their tie bands once likes count. **Dog poop pickup**
goes from the 10-11 band to 6th, on the strength of @Johnrobholmes' two-word
"poo scooper" taking 10 likes -- the highest of any serious reply in the
thread. **Bird feeder refill** goes from the 15-17 band to 12th on @codetaur's
5. Planting slips two places and security one, both on thin numbers. Nothing
else reordered.

**The likes-per-asker split is the more interesting signal**, and it does not
show up as movement. Plant health monitoring drew 4 likes from 7 askers; bird
feeder refill drew 6 from 2. Concrete, physical, slightly funny tasks
over-perform on likes; diagnostic and monitoring tasks under-perform. If likes
are a proxy for what would make people talk about the robot, that is worth
knowing separately from what would make them buy one.

Splitting the rows out sharpens one thing the theme-level view hid: **string
trimming outscores mowing** (20 vs 16), despite mowing having more askers.
Mowing is the thing everyone assumes; string trimming is the thing people are
annoyed about, and it is the job existing robomowers do not do.


## Notes by theme

### Weeding -- rows 1, 10

The runaway winner, and asked for in more detail than anything else. Removal
splits into three jobs sharing a name:

- **Turf weeds.** Dandelions in the lawn. @desmond67569 wants the dandelion
  *isolated* before removal so the seed head does not broadcast on the way out.
- **Bed weeds.** Mulch beds and around ornamentals. @cat4lyst_Ma's stretch goal
  is cultivating *around* plants without damaging them.
- **Crop weeds.** @WolfeTyler: around growing pumpkins (6 likes).

Mechanisms people volunteered: @Rasterdeck25086 is building an arm with a
toothed hole saw that augers the weed to death rather than pulling it, run
often enough to exhaust the root's stored energy -- telescoping, so it reaches
into flower beds. @bliss4kwp wants laser weeding. @HumanSigniture wants a crab
robot that slowly mulches small plants.

Identification is listed separately because two people wanted it as its own
output: @ZECTBynmo wants weeds classified and mapped first and destroyed second
(3 likes), and @desmond67569 wants the dispatch method chosen per species.

Also: @ai_christianson (12 likes, top serious reply), @haydendevs,
@macmaniac77, @patrickdoddboy, @nicolasrenold, @badbirb, @ScottCircuitguy
(bellflower specifically), @The_Essayist_, @Jegber, @dankulous, @reeder1865,
@brianakaka, @masonhensley, @ozcraftpro.

### Mowing, string trimming, edging -- rows 4, 6, 16

Mowing is stated flatly as a given by eight people and draws few likes for it.
String trimming scores higher on fewer askers: @masonhensley wants the weeds
trimmed along the main road his city ignores and says he would pay a monthly
subscription for that today; @ai_christianson lists it first of four;
@dankulous and @brianakaka call it weed eating. @CosmicEggEarth wants the
hard-to-reach places cut -- the part a robomower cannot do. Edging stands alone
from @PhillipsColinG, @reeder1865, @plasmahivecom and @The_Essayist_.

### Debris pickup -- rows 2, 3, 19, 43, 48, 55

Sticks are the single most-wanted object in the thread after weeds.
@geoffdudgeon wants them in a tidy pile and would settle for a pile over
bagging; @danrudolph and @dankulous just want them gone. Leaves are nearly
level (@Eggs_and_Jakey: "picking up leaves one at a time"; @weibiotic wants
them swept and composted). Then the long tail, each from one person: spiky
sweetgum-style nut pods off the sidewalk into a trashcan (@john_henry), stray
rocks tossed back into the gravel path (@asmeias42) and wood chips and gravel
raked back where they belong (@nicolasrenold), kids' toys (@nicolasrenold), and
the piles of clippings the mower leaves behind before the grass under them dies
(@NitoriSmallArms).

### Animals -- rows 8, 22, 26, 28, 42, 51

Squirrels are the arch-enemy: eating herbs (@0ldbury), on the cushions
(@JThomasBurgess), generally (@nicktindle), raiding peaches alongside deer
(@CosmicEggEarth). Deer at night from @badbirb. The rest are detection rather
than deterrence, and are the cheapest things on this page: moles (@nuunien),
gopher holes (@bliss4kwp), the gaps in the fence where rabbits get in
(@ZECTBynmo), and @Mathewdoeslife's "Find the cat" -- 3 likes, one sentence,
and probably the single easiest task anyone named.

### Insects -- rows 9, 25, 29, 34, 53, 61, 64

Ground-nesting stingers dominate, and the motivation is injury avoidance rather
than tidiness: @pagesix1536 wrote in with three fractured ribs from fleeing a
bald-faced hornet nest (3 likes). @AlecSaysThings wants wasp nests knocked down
(4 likes, "been a tough year for that"), @Icalbert101 wants ground bee hives
found and burned (3). Also ticks (@capacitarded), brown widow spiders and ants
(@NeuralYogi), scorpions and mosquitos (@Nana28423123), rattlesnakes
(@seandm69), and @The_Essayist_'s general bug poisoning.

Locating is the hard half. Killing a nest is easy once you know where it is.

### Dog poop -- rows 5, 47, 50

@Johnrobholmes' two-word "poo scooper" took 10 likes, the highest of any
serious reply. @Alevetto07, @dankulous and @tmophoto want the same thing
(@tmophoto suggests a backflip on detection). The enforcement variants are
separate rows because they need no manipulator: @ZECTBynmo wants it recorded
when people leave dog shit or trash on the lawn, @TheJudeBell wants the
neighbour's dogs scared off before the fact.

### Lawn treatment -- rows 13, 15, 21, 36, 54, 59

Aeration is the most-asked (@Jegber, @bliss4kwp, @The_Essayist_,
@1972parzival), then overseeding and fertilizing. @BitShiftRight's
top-dressing is the best fit for this platform of anything in the theme: sand
sprinkled continuously to level the grass and fill low spots gradually, which
only works if the robot visits often. @The_Essayist_ sums the theme up: "100%
hands off lawn care ... I never want to do more than refill the expendables."

### Watering -- row 7

Kept as one row; nobody split it. @Jegber wants precision watering,
@The_Essayist_ adaptive watering, @BitShiftRight watering driven by the robot's
own read of what each plant needs, @badbirb soil moisture as the trigger. Also
@macmaniac77, @nicolasrenold, @ozcraftpro.

### Plant health and sensing -- rows 14, 27, 63

@chrisbolas wants sad plants identified. @cat4lyst_Ma frames it best: don't
diagnose, just compare against the mostly-healthy neighbours and flag "doesn't
look happy", with a photo and a location, for a human. @BitShiftRight wants the
lawn analysed at ~0.5 m^2 resolution with a per-plant treatment plan the robot
decides -- "agentic, not me saying water 3 times/wk". Plus soil moisture and
composition (@badbirb) and @batsuev_es' flat "data collection ofc".

Lowest likes-per-asker of any theme, and still the cheapest to ship -- it is
perception with no actuation.

### Hauling -- rows 17, 30, 32, 45, 46, 65

Firewood restocking (@dankulous, @CosmicEggEarth), logs from the pile to the
basement -- dry ones only, and split them (@CosmicEggEarth), trash bins out and
back (@reeder1865, @R2rule1), mail retrieval (@reeder1865), lawn furniture set
up and put away (@nicolasrenold). @bliss4kwp's towing system and person-
following wheelbarrow is the general form of all of these and would subsume
most of the theme.

### Planting and tree work -- rows 12, 24, 39, 58, plus branch trimming

Planting is generic across five people: roses and raspberry bushes
(@the13olympian), food gardening (@PyrealRat), seeds and a greenhouse floor
(@pinchetobs), planting (@DaveySwift), seeding (@Rasterdeck25086).

The tree rows are all @DisruptionUp, and all specific: meticulous repetitive
arborsculpture -- bending trees a little every day, tying new bendy growth down
weekly, cutting the growths that don't fit the sculpture (3 likes); cloning fig
branches with precise angled cuts below the nodes, dipped in rooting hormone
and set into a clone tank; grafting citrus cuttings onto rootstock.

**Tree branch trimming**, from the other thread, belongs here. It and
arborsculpture want the same hardware -- a positionable cutter on an arm with
enough reach to get above the robot -- and differ only in what decides where to
cut. The repetitive daily-visit framing is a good fit for a resident robot and
a bad fit for a rented lift.

### Cleaning -- rows 35, 41, 44, 49

Mostly @ozcraftpro: stone and wood floors, garden furniture, and his cat's
outdoor toilet. Plus window washing (@CosmicEggEarth). Hard surfaces rather
than lawn, which makes it a different locomotion and sensing problem from
everything above it.

### Spraying -- rows 18, 20

@Praise_Kelechi_ asked for "pesticide/herbicide spraying" in one breath (3
likes) and @The_Essayist_ for weed spraying and bug poisoning, so the two rows
carry identical scores. Thin on asks, but worth noting as the lowest-mechanism
way to act on a weed once you can see one -- no arm, no cutter, just aim and
valve.

### Security -- rows 23, 56, 60

Filtering the shooting jokes leaves three real asks. @ZECTBynmo wants to know
if the same unfamiliar people keep coming around -- a house near him was cased
before being robbed. @bliss4kwp wants the robot as a node in an existing
security system, and mentions child supervision. @Rhobtor wants a dog guardian.

### The singles -- rows 11, 31, 33, 37, 38, 40, 52, 57, 62, 66

**Bird feeder refill** (@codetaur, 5 likes; @dankulous) has the best
likes-per-asker ratio in the thread and is the most bounded job on the page --
known location, known payload, no perception problem worth the name.
**Snow clearing** and **driveway surface cleaning** (@CosmicEggEarth,
@reeder1865, the latter unsure "laser clean" is a real thing) are the only
cold-weather and hard-surface-maintenance asks. **Dog exercise** (@IsZomg,
"chase the fatass dog for 30 mins a day") is phrased as a joke but is a real
job with an unusual shape -- the robot is the stimulus, so it needs speed and a
dog-safe body and almost no precision. **Bird call identification**
(@chrisbolas) is passive sensing that runs while the robot does something else;
with more than one bot you could triangulate the caller. **Foraging**
(@OffgridAlaskan) is the same perception stack pointed at a much harder target,
well outside a yard. **Gutter cleaning** and **Christmas lights** (@NeuralYogi)
both need to get off the ground, and he tags them himself as "high risk
activities" -- the strongest justification anyone offered for any task here.
**Trail or fire line cutting** (@lgwalker2010) is property-scale, and closer to
the mowing hardware than anything else. **Rainwater collection install**
(@TheColonyAI) is excavation, not yard work; he asked "or is that bigger than
you are thinking?" It is.


## Cross-cutting requirements

Not tasks, but stated as assumptions by several people:

- **Self-charging and self-sufficiency.** @BitShiftRight wants water and
  sand/dirt access plus auto-charge. @Cyborganic_27: "all of them. then charge
  itself. i want to not have to tell you."
- **Scheduling and unattended operation.** Repeatedly implied -- the value is
  in the robot visiting often, not in doing any one pass well.
- **Report, don't only act.** Several of the best-formed asks (rabbit gaps,
  gopher holes, unhappy plants, weed maps, the cat, dog-poop offenders) are
  pure detect-and-tell. No manipulator needed.


## Reading of it

Perception is the common substrate. Weed removal, stick and leaf pickup, poop
pickup, plant distress, and every animal and insect row are all "find the small
thing in the grass and know what it is" -- that is most of the top twenty, and
the existing plant-segmentation work feeds all of them.

Manipulation splits cleanly by force. Sticks, nuts, rocks, toys and poop are
one low-force gripper problem and cover six rows. Weeding, string trimming,
mowing, edging, trimming and pruning are cutting problems. Firewood and logs
are a different machine. Cleaning, gutters and Christmas lights want a platform
this one is not.

The lowest-effort high-signal shipment is still a detect-and-report pass --
weed map, unhappy plants, nest locations, where the cat is -- because it needs
no actuator and satisfies a real chunk of what people asked for. Counting likes
does not change that, but it adds a caveat: monitoring is what people want and
not what they cheer for. If the first public demo needs to land, the poop
scooper and the bird feeder are the crowd-pleasers.
